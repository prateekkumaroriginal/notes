Clinical Specialist Remote X-ray Review and Clinical Assessment

01

The two workflows

TB screening camps send images for Remote X-ray Review. The Clinical Specialist uses Clinical Assessment & Diagnosis to record a beneficiary’s clinical outcome.

Remote X-ray Review

Some digital X-ray devices save images only on a separate laptop. Camps often work offline, and remote specialists cannot access the laptop or camp server.

  1. The camp user uploads images against a Beneficiary ID.
  2. The camp server syncs them to the central PACS when online. An online uploader can send them directly. The case becomes Available.
  3. The system can notify active specialists mapped to the camp.
  4. A specialist signs in and opens the Pending list.
  5. The specialist opens the case, reviews the images in the DICOM viewer, saves annotations if needed, and reports.
  6. The system attaches the signed report to the beneficiary record and syncs it back to the camp.
  7. The camp user gets a notification in the TB screening application. The system highlights urgent or TB-suggestive results.

Clinical Assessment & Diagnosis

The TBMJA application lists beneficiaries who meet the assessment rules, including eligible beneficiaries without a chest X-ray.

  1. The field team records screening and any available test results.
  2. TBMJA automatically adds eligible beneficiaries to Pending → Initial.
  3. The Clinical Specialist signs in, opens the Clinical Assessment card, and selects a beneficiary.
  4. The specialist reads the Beneficiary Clinical Summary, diagnostics, and available CXR evidence.
  5. The specialist records an Assessment Outcome and any Recommended Advice, or requests a retake if the CXR is inadequate.
  6. TBMJA routes the case to Completed, Pending → Re-review, a Referral list, or the Confirmed TB Cases list according to the outcome and advice.
Shared form. Remote Review FR-46 refers to the TBMJA section Clinical Assessment Form → Review of diagnostic evidence. A remote X-ray report and a Clinical Assessment record have different routing rules. See Open integration decisions.

02

Access and worklists

Remote Review limits access by camp mapping. TBMJA limits the Clinical Assessment line listing to the specialist’s mapped location.

Remote Review enrollment and sign-in

  • FR-01–03 · Must Admin enrolls specialists in Admin-UI → Employee Master. Name, phone, email, WhatsApp number and place are required. MCI/NMC registration number, qualification, place of work, specialisation and configurable fields are optional enrollment fields. Validate email and 10-digit Indian mobile and WhatsApp numbers. Reject duplicate phone or email.
  • FR-04–05, FR-08 · Must Admin whitelists the registered phone and email, issues a username and temporary password, and sends a welcome message. The doctor changes the password at first sign-in. Only Admin can change whitelisted contacts.
  • FR-06–07, FR-09 · Must Admin can map each doctor to multiple camps and each camp to multiple doctors. Admin can change mappings or deactivate and reactivate accounts. Deactivation ends active sessions and removes case access immediately. Profile details supply the report sign-off.
  • FR-10–12, FR-16 · Must Sign-in uses username or whitelisted phone/email, password, and a six-digit OTP sent to the registered mobile. Configurable defaults are five minutes of validity, three wrong attempts, and up to three resends at intervals of at least 30 seconds. Repeated failures lock the account for a configurable period and alert Admin. Password reset uses an OTP to the registered mobile.
  • FR-13 · Must Token-based sessions have configurable idle and maximum durations. Fifteen minutes is an example idle timeout. Auto-save unsaved report drafts before timeout.
  • FR-14–15 · Could Admin can allow device biometrics via WebAuthn/passkeys per doctor. The doctor first authenticates by password and OTP. Password and OTP remain available, and biometric data stays on the device.

Remote Review queue and notifications

  • FR-29–30 · Must A central case is visible only to active specialists mapped to its camp. Opening it locks reporting to that specialist. The lock expires after a configurable idle period if they do not submit.
  • FR-31–33 · Must Pending, Re-review and Completed each show a count and support sorting and filtering by camp, upload date, Beneficiary ID, urgency and case age. A retake upload, a camp clarification or a specialist flag puts the case in Re-review.
  • FR-34–36 · Should Optional browser push or SMS follows the specialist’s preference. Batch alerts by camp and exclude beneficiary names and clinical details. Send reminders after the configurable turnaround target. The target triggers reminders only.

TBMJA role and automatic listing

Use the existing application, login, and online and offline modes. The Clinical Specialist can be an authorised Medical Officer or above, a chest physician, a radiologist, or another designated specialist. The role can view the line listing, Beneficiary Clinical Summary and CXR viewer. It can view, add and edit an assessment until submission.

The role has no access to Household, Non-Household, Registration, Admin, Counselling, Contact Tracing or TPT. It has no access to data entry for Anthropometry, General Examination, TB Screening, General OPD or Referral.

TBMJA lists a beneficiary automatically if any of these rules apply:

  • The beneficiary has an abnormal CXR and a negative or unavailable Truenat result, whether or not they have TB symptoms.
  • The beneficiary has TB symptoms or risk factors, could not undergo CXR, and has a negative or unavailable Truenat result. Risk factors include previous TB with current symptoms, a close household contact with TB, smoking, COPD and HIV. Reasons CXR may be unavailable include pregnancy, paediatric age, and clinical or operational constraints.
  • The beneficiary is a child aged 3–14 years. This rule lists the child directly for Clinical Assessment.

Home shows only the Clinical Assessment card and Dashboard tab. The card count adds Pending and Completed. Each tab shows its own count and supports oldest or newest listing date and name A–Z. The card and Pending default to oldest first. Completed defaults to newest first.

  • Pending → Initial contains new cases that need assessment.
  • Pending → Re-review contains cases that need another assessment, such as inconclusive cases and inadequate-image submissions.
  • Completed contains closed cases. The outcome and advice rules specify which submissions close a case.

03

Image upload, storage and viewer

The Remote Review uploader is an extension of MMU-UI. The central PACS supplies DICOM images to the specialist viewer.

Upload and sync

  • FR-17–18 · Must On the X-ray laptop, the radiographer or camp coordinator searches for and selects the Beneficiary ID. Each upload can include multiple images, the view or body part, and clinical notes.
  • FR-19–22 · Must Route offline uploads to the camp server and online uploads directly to the central server. Accept DICOM files. Convert JPEG and PNG exports to DICOM secondary capture with the Beneficiary ID and study details in the header. Validate type and size, warn on DICOM Patient ID mismatch, and support resumable chunked uploads with per-image progress and result.
  • FR-23 · Should Allow the camp user to delete or replace the image until a specialist opens the case.
  • FR-24–27 · Must Queue images on the local server and sync automatically when connectivity returns, with retries and duplicate prevention. The central server stores DICOM and preserves study, series and instance metadata. Provide DICOMweb storage, query and retrieval through STOW-RS, QIDO-RS and WADO-RS. Create a central case linked to the beneficiary after all images in the upload arrive.
  • FR-28 · Should Show the camp user whether the upload is queued at the local server or synced.

View and annotate

  • FR-37–38 · Must Show Beneficiary ID, age, sex, camp, X-ray date, view and clinical notes. Mask name and contact details. Stream from the central PACS to a browser viewer such as OHIF on desktop, phone and tablet.
  • FR-39–40 · Must Viewer tools include zoom, pan, window/level, invert, rotate/flip, length and angle measurement, and full screen. Annotation tools include arrows, freehand drawing, ellipse and rectangle regions, text, and measurement markings.
  • FR-41–42 · Must Save each annotation version under the original image. Keep the original and earlier versions intact. Record version number, specialist, date and time, and an optional note.
  • FR-43–45 · Should Store annotations in PACS in a DICOM-compatible form such as Structured Report, Presentation State or a derived secondary capture. Let specialists compare earlier X-rays and annotation versions. Disable download and print by default and watermark views with the specialist’s name and time.

04

Beneficiary record and diagnostics

The Clinical Assessment screen reads information from existing TBMJA modules. The Clinical Specialist stores decisions in a separate Clinical Assessment record. Diagnostic test results stay read-only.

Beneficiary card

The card shows status icons for TB Symptoms, CXR and NAAT. Read-only registration data includes full name, age in years, gender, AMRIT ID, head of family, father or husband name, and phone number. Hide the phone if it is the default 9999999999. Show the Nikshay ID if available. Show village and the recorded area, such as Tribal-NPVTG, Tribal-PVTG, Rural, Urban, Urban Slum or Unknown.

Opening the card shows three collapsible parts in TBMJA. These are Clinical Summary, Diagnostics and CXR image viewer, and Clinical Assessment Form.

TBMJA PRD

05

Reporting, re-review and results

Remote Review returns a signed image report to the camp. TBMJA records the Assessment Outcome and routes the beneficiary according to that outcome and any Recommended Advice.

Remote Review report

  • FR-46–48 · Must Use the TBMJA diagnostic evidence form structure. Record findings, impression, normal or abnormal, TB-suggestive status (yes, no or indeterminate), other findings, recommendation, urgency (routine or urgent), and the annotation version used. Sign-off uses the specialist’s name, MCI/NMC number, contact details, place and submission time.
  • FR-49–50 · Must Save a draft and submit later. Submitted reports are read-only. For an unreadable image, record the reason and request a camp retake. The retake upload puts the case in Re-review. See the FR-50 wording issue.
  • FR-51–52 · Should Correct submitted reports with an addendum that preserves the original. Generate a PDF with the annotated image version and sign-off.
  • FR-53–56 · Must Attach the report to the beneficiary. Sync it to the camp server and TB screening application. Notify the camp user and make the report viewable. Highlight urgent or TB-suggestive results and notify the camp immediately.

Dashboards and service requirements

Remote Review measures camp uploads and specialist turnaround. TBMJA tracks clinical assessments and their outcomes.

Remote Review operations

  • FR-57 · Must Audit enrollment, mapping, login, OTP, upload, sync, case open, annotation save, report submit and notification. Record user, time and device or IP address.
  • FR-58 · Should Give supervisors or Admin a dashboard for cases by status and camp, time pending, and turnaround by specialist.

Proposed success measures include the share of X-rays in PACS with a valid Beneficiary ID, zero unauthorised access incidents, median time from Available to report submission, the share of cases with a current status, and the share of annotated cases with versions linked to the original image. The spec leaves measure targets to confirm.

07

Acceptance checks and dependencies

Use these checks to read the workflow from camp upload to result return, and from TBMJA listing to clinical outcome.

Remote Review acceptance criteria

  1. Admin can enroll a doctor only if all five required fields are present. Optional fields can be blank.
  2. Reject sign-in with a non-whitelisted phone or email. A whitelisted doctor signs in with the correct password and OTP. An idle session ends and requires sign-in again.
  3. On a supported device, a doctor can register and use face or fingerprint sign-in. Password and OTP still work.
  4. An offline camp can upload three X-rays against one Beneficiary ID. Once the camp comes online, all three arrive as DICOM in the central PACS.
  5. A mapped specialist sees the Pending case and gets a notification if enabled. A specialist without the camp mapping cannot see or open it.
  6. When one specialist opens the case, other mapped specialists see that it is In review and cannot report on it.
  7. Two saves create annotation versions 1 and 2 under the unchanged original.
  8. Submission moves the case to Completed, signs the report with the doctor’s profile details, and notifies the camp user.
  9. An unreadable report creates a retake request. The new upload returns the case to Re-review.
  10. The audit log records the actions in the workflow.

TBMJA walkthrough

These checks follow the PRD workflow. Start with a qualifying beneficiary and follow the record through the Home card, line listing, clinical evidence and assessment. Check inadequate CXR, each Assessment Outcome, and each Recommended Advice option against the routing rules. Confirm that submission leaves the source test results intact.

Remote Review assumptions and dependencies

  • The device can export DICOM, JPEG or PNG to the laptop. The laptop can reach the camp local server using the camp network. Beneficiary IDs exist before or during the camp.
  • Specialists use a modern browser. The central service needs a PACS and a web viewer that supports annotations.
  • Camp operations depend on the existing local server sync service. Notification and enrollment flows depend on the approved SMS gateway, email service and web push.

08

Open integration decisions

The specs agree on the need for a CXR review. These points need a decision before the two workflows can share records or screens.

How does the remote report enter Clinical Assessment?
FR-46 reuses the diagnostic evidence form structure. FR-53 sends the report to the beneficiary and camp. The PRD reads the CXR result from Referral and stores its own Clinical Assessment record. The pages leave open whether the remote report appears as read-only evidence, supplies any prefilled fields, or triggers a separate assessment.
Who can see each view?
Remote Review requires whitelisted contacts, MFA, camp mapping, and masked names and contacts. TBMJA uses its existing login and mapped location. Its beneficiary card includes the name and phone when available. Confirm which access and display rules apply when a user moves between views.
When can a specialist see a retake case again?
Remote Review keeps it off the specialist list until the retake arrives. TBMJA lists it in Pending → Re-review immediately after the inadequate-quality submission. FR-50 also says “the Quality of CXR is adequate” where the Remote Review status table and acceptance criteria describe an unreadable image. Confirm the field label and trigger.
Which fields are available without a CXR?
TBMJA lists eligible cases without a CXR. Its form text enables the Review of diagnostic evidence section only when a CXR is available, but places Assessment Outcome and Recommended Advice in that section. It also refers to “CXR image reviewed” without defining a separate field in the table. Confirm the available fields and review action.
What happens when Recommended Advice gives two routes?
TBMJA supports multiple options. A higher-centre referral closes the assessment, while repeat CXR, NAAT and other investigations return it to Re-review. The PRD leaves the result of selecting both types together open.
Which notification channels are in scope?
Remote Review FR-34 names browser push and SMS. The workflow diagram also shows WhatsApp. The written requirements leave that channel open.
